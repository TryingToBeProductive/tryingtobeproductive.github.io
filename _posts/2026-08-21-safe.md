It is morning. You are in a hurry. Maybe because you overslept, or maybe because you need to put the final touch on your project. Either way, you speed up. You start running instead of walking. You pedal your bicycle faster. And then you wonder why you don't always get to your destination faster. Well, it is tiring, but you will get used to it eventually. Furthermore, it cuts down on transit time and gives us more time to do real work. But we don't because the actual reason for not commuting faster is safety.

The more you run, the more likely you are to trip, fall, or crash into someone. Drive or ride at higher speeds, and accidents become more serious. We don't have to actively pay attention to our safety to moderate our actions because this happens unconsciously.

If we expand our understanding of safety beyond preventing physical injury, we can start talking about risks as well. Tasks, deadlines, and projects all have their own risks, whether we do them or not. Some may be so small that they barely matter, while others can change your career. When trying to be productive, we tend to focus on speed, precision, and efficiency, but risk management has to be considered if we want to achieve the desired outcome.

The easiest and most intuitive way to incorporate safety measures into your productivity system is through prioritization, specifically by considering importance rather than urgency. The more important something is to you, the more likely the risk of not doing it will cause you trouble in the future. Urgency, however, incentivizes speed, which defeats the whole point of risk management.

Of course, focusing only on what has to be done will result in overpreparation. You will be equipped for everything, but you will have spent a significant amount of time and mental energy preparing for every possibility. To avoid this trap, the importance of one task has to be considered in relation to others. How important is it to not work on \_\_\_\_\_ so that you can give more time to \_\_\_\_\_? What is the risk of doing \_\_\_\_\_ on \_\_\_\_\_? What do I lose by committing to \_\_\_\_\_ instead of not committing to \_\_\_\_\_? Prioritization entails saying yes to one and saying no to many. Therefore, to better manage your risks, knowing the value of inaction, similar to walking instead of running, is equally important.

Safety is one of the most underappreciated parts of our daily lives. Humanity has built so many institutions just to keep ourselves free from unnecessary pain and suffering. Just as we create societal systems with safety in mind, we should also build our productivity systems to manage our risks. The first step is to evaluate tasks and their relationships to one another so that we can prioritize both action and inaction. The faster the speed, the harder the crash. To avoid erasing all your work because of a small mistake, value safety.
