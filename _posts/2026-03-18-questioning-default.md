*Estimated Reading Time : 3-4min*

Browsers, search engines, and office software are largely dominated by a few products: Chromium-based browsers, Google, and Microsoft 365. There are plenty of alternatives—both free and premium—that could better serve our needs. However, those who use them are still a minority because, as companies already understand, moving beyond the default requires a significant amount of energy. There are two main factors that keep us from changing: our biological tendency to conserve energy and the high activation energy required to switch. As a result, similar to a Boltzmann distribution, most people remain as they are, while only a few receive enough push to do otherwise.

Even though we cannot remove these evolutionary tendencies (yet), there are “enzymes” that can mentally lower the activation energy. The catalyst is [questioning](/2026/03/16/asking-right-questions/)—specifically, questioning the default. It can be as simple as asking, “Why am I using or doing this?” or “Am I doing this simply because others are doing it?” If you want to go further, you can ask, “Is there a better way?” By shining light on our subconscious habits, we can uncover hidden friction, artificial limitations, or unnecessary dependencies that hold us back. Of course, it may initially feel uncomfortable to do things differently from the majority. However, as long as you do not draw attention to it, most people either do not care or are too preoccupied with their own [priorities](/2026/03/05/priorities/). As a result, you can enjoy the benefits without worrying about feeling like an outlier.

We should still keep in mind that there are reasons why things are the way they are. Sometimes the reason is simple, such as business [incentives](/2026/08/24/incentives/); other times, it is more complex, such as a solution that works best for a problem with many variables. If we are not careful, the latter can easily be misjudged as “old habits” and dismissed prematurely. Mistakes are inevitable, so the best approach is to correct them quickly. If you keep records before making changes to your daily systems, it becomes much easier to identify decision errors and revert to a previous setup if necessary.

Status Quo Bias is often invisible but has a strong effect on how we think and act. It can be used intentionally by corporations to maximize profits or unintentionally by ourselves to conserve energy. To remove this hidden drag, we need to question defaults in the first place. Of course, we may sometimes misjudge their importance, but if we keep records, we can adjust and refine our decisions over time. An increase in performance starts with tinkering with the default system. When the settings are adjusted for maximum productivity, we can reach our goals more efficiently.
