Some people claim that today’s high school students are “smarter” than the geniuses of the 17th century. Of course, in terms of raw intellectual ability, figures like Isaac Newton or Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz would easily surpass most modern 18-year-olds. Yet many students today can learn calculus, Newtonian physics, chemistry, and astronomy—subjects that were once understood by only a small elite. How is this possible?

The answer lies in tools.

We now have extensive educational infrastructure that accelerates [learning](/2026/03/17/educational-content/): structured curricula, schools, textbooks, videos, and standardized assessments. Beyond physical resources, we also rely on conceptual tools—analogies, examples, and step-by-step explanations—that act as ladders to higher understanding. Tools make what was once inaccessible attainable for the majority.

When deciding our next course of action, we typically begin by revisiting our goals and visualizing the desired outcome. From there, we consider the most efficient path forward. At this stage, the tools we possess—or lack—can significantly influence our chances of success. Having multiple methods available reduces friction, especially at the beginning of a project. At the same time, we can always search for better tools: cheaper ones, faster ones, simpler ones, or more powerful ones. Identifying and applying the right tools can save time, [money](/2026/03/22/budgeting/), and cognitive effort.

Importantly, tools are not limited to physical objects such as hardware or equipment. They can also be intangible: mindsets, frameworks, protocols, or system maps. These are often easier to acquire and maintain, as they require little space and minimal upkeep, and can be stored in notebooks or digital tools. However, integrating them can be challenging, especially when they are highly specialized. Rather than forcing everything into a single, unified system, it is often more effective to switch frameworks depending on the situation. This flexibility allows each tool to be used to its full potential.

Tools do not come first—but they almost always come second. When we encounter limitations, tools allow us to overcome constraints and achieve what initially seemed impossible. They exist in many forms, and their effectiveness depends on how well they match the situation. When used appropriately, their precision can be surprisingly powerful.

The next time you plan a project, think carefully about the tools available to you. The right tool may not only accelerate your progress, but fundamentally change your productivity.
