Have you ever used airplane mode on your phone or PC? If you have been on a plane before, you probably have. But what about using it on land with Wi-Fi turned off? When I tried it for the first time, of course I felt disconnected, but I also felt liberated—as if digital chains had been broken. You might experience something similar when you fully go offline. Even when your devices are in sleep mode, your subconscious mind may still feel connected to the internet because we all know, deep down, that we are only one swipe away from accessing it. Once that entrance is sealed, we can cut the digital strings that have been wrapping around our minds and regain a sense of autonomy.

However, many of our tasks nowadays rely on internet access. Yet when examined carefully, we could technically accomplish many of them using only our brains and offline materials. Definite exceptions would include online transactions that require live data exchange, such as digital payments, geographically isolated collaboration, or meetings on Zoom. Other activities—such as researching, coding, writing, or consuming content—can often be done offline simply by downloading what you need beforehand. Videos and [music](/2026/03/19/audio-content/) can be saved as MP4 and MP3 files. Documents, spreadsheets, slides, and PDFs can be edited without an internet connection. Even when you get stuck and need answers, you can search offline versions of resources like [Wikipedia](/2026/04/23/wikipedia/) or Stack Overflow through tools such as Kiwix. If you review your to-do list carefully and prepare everything in advance, many tasks can be completed without opening your browser even once. The only thing you may need to worry about is storage space.

Unfortunately, there is one tool that is still difficult to use fully offline: large language models. Of course, as time goes on, increases in CPU or GPU processing speed and improvements in model architecture may allow individuals to run powerful offline assistants on their own devices. But for now, the best most people can do is either use a smaller model locally or access a stronger model through an API while minimizing distractions. Another option is simply relying on your own thinking and scripts, which may actually increase mental engagement even if it slows things down. If your workflow depends heavily on large language models, some [compromise](/2026/04/02/trade-offs/) may be necessary.

To have complete control of our contious mind, self-imposed internet blackout is necessary. Once our brains are free, we could fully use them as we please. Achieving this requires preparation and a few compromises. But once everything is set up,true productivity will be unlocked.
