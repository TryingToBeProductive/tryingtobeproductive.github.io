It goes without saying that advertisements are annoying. They interrupt your flow, promote consumerist behavior, and clutter your mind with meaningless information. There is no need to convince you that they harm your performance. I can also skip the moral arguments about ads, because if you are not going to click them anyway, you neither make the provider richer nor poorer by simply viewing them. [Blocking](/2026/02/24/blocking-distractions/) these disruptors is easy; the hard part is building a system that is almost all-encompassing.

The first and most powerful filter is a private [DNS](/2026/03/03/managing-private-dns/). If your device cannot access ads, they cannot be shown on your screen. There are plenty of public DNS services focused primarily on blocking advertisements, pop-ups, and even malware. In addition, you can add your own lists to expand the shield. You can also block app-specific advertisements by monitoring the requests an app makes within the first few seconds of opening it and identifying which domains are responsible. DNS gives you flexible and effective control, making it a great starting point for protecting your productivity.

The second line of defense is your browser or browser extensions. A quick search will point you to options like Brave or uBlock Origin. Try installing them and using them during both work and downtime. These tools help filter out ads and malware that are difficult for DNS alone to block—especially when they are delivered through large platforms like Google or Meta, which have the resources to bypass simple filters. Of course, when these companies become restrictive, they may limit access for users who block their ads. In such cases, you can either switch to alternatives or access the same content through indirect means.

The final layer of protection is using open-source applications. Most open-source projects do not insert ads into their interfaces. Not only do they provide tools comparable to mainstream applications, but they sometimes allow you to view content without directly visiting ad-heavy websites. This helps you avoid being bombarded with distractions. The user interface may be less polished, as many open-source tools prioritize functionality over design. However, if ads and pop-ups are consistently disrupting your workflow, spending time learning these tools can be worthwhile in the long run.

Time, [money](/2026/03/22/budgeting/), energy, and attention are all limited resources. The last thing we want is for them to be wasted on constant advertising. By surrounding yourself with a digital fortress, you can protect your productivity and maintain more consistent performance—both now and in the future.
