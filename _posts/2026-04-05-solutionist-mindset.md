Nowadays, techno-optimists tend to be criticized for their tendency to oversimplify societal issues and promote investment in new technologies instead of improving existing [tools](/2026/04/27/tools/). These critiques are valid, and we need to be aware of them. As the saying goes, “To a man with a hammer, everything looks like a nail.”

Still, there are reasons why this ideology became popular in the first place. One of them is its solutionist mindset. Rather than fixating on problems, it encourages us to focus on solving them. The appeal of this approach is that it connects directly to output, which is usually the end product of most projects. In addition, when this mindset is separated from the broader movement, it forces us to work with the tools and materials currently available, instead of relying on future innovations.

Solutionist thinking shows its true strength during the planning phase. When setting [goals](/2026/02/13/goal-setting/), there are often hidden obstacles—either physical or mental—that we need to overcome. By identifying the underlying challenge and defining your objective as solving it, you have effectively mapped out both the starting point and the end goal of your journey. The remaining steps can then be divided into research and action, which can be adjusted based on these two points.

Nevertheless, we still need to keep in mind the pitfalls that some sociologists have pointed out—namely, complexity. There are conflicts, both internal and external, that are too complex to solve with individually designed systems alone. We need to be aware of our locus of control. No matter how hard we try, most of us cannot control the weather, the economy, our biochemistry, geopolitics, and many other factors. When problems stem from larger structures, learning to live along side them is often a more productive use of time than trying to change reality itself (unless that is your job—in which case, good luck).

Wearing a solutionist “hat” can improve output by keeping us focused on moving from problems to solutions. However, this mindset still has to contend with individual limitations. In such cases, we need to shift toward coexistence rather than control, so we don’t waste resources on problems that cannot be solved. Use the solutionist thinking depending on the sacle of the issue at hand to improve performance where we can and defend your performance where we cannot.
