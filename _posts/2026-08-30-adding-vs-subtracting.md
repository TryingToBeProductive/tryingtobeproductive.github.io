*Estimated Reading Time : 3-4min*

If you skim through your [goals](/2026/02/13/goal-setting/), you might notice that most of them are either "Do X more" or "Do Y less." When reviewing our own lives, we might find ourselves being a maximalist in some aspects while being a minimalist in others. Of course, we also have processes that end up at zero, such as replacing our tools by throwing away the old ones and buying new ones.

Adding and subtracting are the most basic concepts in algebra. However, when used as a framework, they can organize lists of tasks, work, desires, and objectives cleanly without oversimplifying or neglecting the details. They become especially helpful when untangling the relationships between multiple ongoing projects, [needs and wants](/2026/05/01/need-vs-want/), and the factors behind all of them.

Since the phrases "Do X more" or "Do Y less" can be reused time and time again simply by replacing X or Y with specific arrangements of words, you can still focus on the details while processing the bigger picture: "Is this an addition or a subtraction?" To use intentionality as an example, you can write, "Be more focused on the task at hand during work hours" or "Be less distracted by time-wasters while using the computer." To describe the factors and desires behind them, you can further write, "My browser has been making it easier and easier to access news" or "I want to be less disappointed by my [time tracking](/2026/02/09/time-management/) results." Once you understand the cause, you can come up with tasks or long-term projects such as "Develop scripts that make news sites less accessible" or "Add more layers of backup that prevent even the administrator from disabling the [block](/2026/02/24/blocking-distractions/)."

If you want to go even further, you can use "Less" or "More" as your yearly theme as well. As presented in the last paragraph, the details can be added later depending on your inner and outer context. If you are in the "Year of Less," you can interpret it as meaning less stress, waste, tiredness, clutter, and so on. On the other hand, the "Year of More" can mean more income, happiness, hobbies, focus, and so on. As long as you use the theme as a guide rather than as a set of goals, you should be able to deploy this broad framework to address everyday problems.

Nowadays, binary thinking is usually discouraged because of its loss of [nuance](/2026/05/03/nuance/). However, mathematics contains both abstract theorems and practical examples, allowing us to move back and forth between [abstraction](/2026/08/19/abstract-thinking/) and application. By exploiting these features as frameworks, we can bypass some of the limitations of conventional binary thinking and start decoding our motivations and actions. Categorization is unavoidable when building a productivity system. So use basic concepts from algebra initially to find solutions for complex problems later on.
