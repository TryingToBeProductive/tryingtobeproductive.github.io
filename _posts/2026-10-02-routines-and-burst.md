You are starting a new project. It will take a few months to complete, but most of the work is either straightforward or repetitive. So you turn it into a [routine](/2026/02/20/routines/), doing it every day for 30 minutes. Yet, by that logic, you realize that you could gain a month’s worth of progress by doing it for 14 hours straight on [weekends](/2026/08/17/working-on-holidays/). Therefore, you might wonder: should you blast through large projects or work toward them incrementally?

A routine is a form of long-term commitment. Even though the friction mostly disappears once it becomes a habit, it is unavoidable that 30 minutes out of the 24 hours in your day will always be blocked off. On the other hand, doing it in large chunks does offer more flexibility. However, if the project requires some amount of skill development or workflow optimization along the way, trying to accomplish it within a few days would be less efficient. It is hard to beat plain old spaced repetition and trial and error.

Now that we understand both strengths and weaknesses, how can we have the best of both worlds? It might be cliché, but by using both.

During the initial period, you use the repetition and time limits that routines provide to build muscle memory and perfect the process. During your project, you inevitably come across days when you can spend 8 or 10 hours just on the project. When that day arrives, use your experience from the routine to blast through the work efficiently and make significant progress. After that, you could either continue with the routine until the next [opportunity](/2026/04/07/opportunity/) arrives, or, if it is near completion, spend a bit more time to finish it for good.

When we become strict with our routines, we tend to forget that some of them can be done without them. Routines pay dividends; that is why they are so attractive. But we could also reinvest those dividends to accelerate our growth. Our schedules cannot always be consistent, which also means that rare opportunities can arise from time to time. Be prepared to snatch them; they might become an unexpected productivity boost.
