Before task managers, there were to-do lists. I think we could all agree that most of us were introduced to productivity hacks through to-do lists. Even if we were able to keep track of upcoming tasks when we were younger, as we grow older, both the number of short-term and long-term tasks increases, and they become more complicated. The advantage of to-do lists is that they are simple yet flexible, allowing anyone to use them in any context. So even if each of us is living in different circumstances, you cannot go wrong with starting your productivity journey with a to-do list.

A to-do list consists of only two parts: a checkbox and a short task description. As a result, it can be written on a piece of paper, in an app, in a document, or even on a napkin. Once you have completed the tasks, you can throw away the entire list, archive it, or keep it temporarily. There is no correct way to manage your to-do list. Whether it increases your productivity is all that matters.

If you want to level up your to-do list into something closer to a task manager, you can build systems on top of or around your existing list. If you are looking for something more sophisticated, you can start researching keywords like “GTD (Getting Things Done).” Even though the arrival of email has changed the original system, there are many personalized versions of GTD that you can use as inspiration to build or improve your own task manager.

As you research, you might notice some common core components. Tasks can generally be divided into three categories. The first is one-time tasks that must be completed within a certain timeframe. The second is recurring tasks that should be done every few days, weeks, or months. The third consists of tasks that may or may not be tackled [someday](/2026/04/05/dealing-with-completionist-mindset/) but need to be written down to clear your mind. There are many ways to organize tasks based on these categories, and you probably already have an idea in mind. Try implementing it, stress-test it, and if you find weaknesses, refine it until you are satisfied.

Most likely, your to-do list or task manager will remain a long-term work in progress as your internal self and external environment change. However, as long as the [foundation](/2026/09/15/foundation-building/) is maintained and strengthened, you will be able to sustain your productivity over time.
