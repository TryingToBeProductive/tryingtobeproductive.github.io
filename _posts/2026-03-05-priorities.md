We are social creatures. Whether we like it or not, we constantly compare ourselves with others. You have probably heard the advice many times: “Don’t compare your worst with someone else’s best. It’s not productive.” But when you think about it, why are some people skilled or talented in certain areas while being inept in others? Some differences are too complex to be explained simply by “genes.” There is another explanation: priorities.

As long as people do not start bending space-time, we all have the same number of hours in a day. Of course, the wealthy can sometimes “buy time” through delegation, but even that has limits. If everyone has the same amount of time, then how we spend it makes the difference. Over long periods, the total hours invested in practicing a skill or refining a technique can determine one’s level of mastery. Even fast learners eventually have to slow down to truly master the final 20%.

So if we want to be “better” than others, should we simply allocate more time to what they are already good at? In principle, that logic is correct. But the real question is whether that is what you should prioritize. Everyone has different goals that drive their choices. It is highly unlikely that your goals are identical to someone else’s, so placing more importance on what others value instead of what you value makes little sense. It is far more productive to view comparisons as glimpses of alternative outcomes—paths that might have emerged if you had chosen different priorities.

However, deciding what to prioritize is not always easy, especially when you have competing interests. Even if you have a single goal, there are often multiple ways to pursue it. Sometimes we also have several unrelated goals because human beings are multidimensional. In such situations, having a limited amount of time may actually be beneficial. You can treat the 24 hours of a day as a boundary frame and your commitments as pieces of a jigsaw puzzle. If we could, we might want to do everything and become everything. Yet when faced with constraints, we are forced to choose.

Priorities guide our lives and shape our talents. To achieve some of our [goals](/2026/02/13/goal-setting/) within a limited timeframe, we must intentionally decide how to spend our time. When priorities are understood and used wisely, productivity naturally follows.
