*Estimated Reading Time : 4-5min*

“Internet was a mistake.” You may have thought that before — especially when you get sucked into a vortex engineered by geniuses in Silicon Valley. You may have heard discussions about the “attention economy.” You may have watched the documentary *The Social Dilemma*, which warns about its dangers. Whether we like it or not, most of us are aware of how destructive distractions are to our daily productivity.

As you quickly realize, willpower alone is not enough to overcome them. The world’s largest corporations have invested enormous amounts of resources to get us hooked and keep us on their platforms as long as possible to show us ads. In such cases — an eye for an eye — we need to use software and programs to fight back against those very same systems.

Depending on your level of addiction, the method can be mild or extreme. Let’s start simple. One of the easiest yet effective solutions is creating your own private [DNS](/2026/03/03/managing-private-dns/). You can use services such as NextDNS or Pi-hole to build a block list of unproductive websites. You can also add community-made lists to block ads, malware, and hidden productivity traps. The advantage of DNS-level blocking is that it prevents easy bypassing. You may have noticed that typical app blockers do not block websites accessed through non-browser apps. However, when DNS access is restricted, the only way around it is to disable the DNS itself.

For those who are easily tempted to disable their own anti-distraction systems, blocking access to the settings themselves may be necessary. Any app requires access to settings to be enabled or disabled — so why not make those settings inaccessible? For most people, myself included, we do not constantly adjust system settings, so losing access to them is not a major issue. There are many premium and freemium apps that can accomplish this effectively. Try installing at least two (in case one fails) and block access to the settings for a week. Then observe how much you actually need them.

For computers, the situation becomes trickier, especially if you are the administrator with full control. In such cases, intentionally building your system to be difficult to dismantle might be the only solution. With the help of generative AI, you can create more complex scripts or programs that take time and effort to remove but still function properly. The more codes you write and the more unconventional system is the harder it becomes to impulsively bypass your own safeguards.

It takes time to set up [tools](/2026/04/27/tools/) that ultimately save time. But you cannot keep letting already wealthy tech companies profit from your stolen attention. Experiment if necessary. Research if needed. Build an environment that protects your focus and allows you to reach your full potential.
