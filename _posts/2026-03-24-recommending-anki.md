If you were a student, you may have heard of a software called Anki. You may also be familiar with “spaced repetition,” a technique that helps you retain information with minimal time investment. As you might expect, Anki (open-sourced) is designed to show you flashcards at the optimal time to reinforce memory. At first glance, this may seem more like a study trick than a productivity tool. However, when applied to the right goals, it can significantly accelerate a large part of the process.

A typical scenario would be working toward being accepted as something—someone with recognized skills, a reliable problem-solver, or even a naturalized citizen. In many cases, the fastest way to reach such [goals](/2026/02/13/goal-setting/) is to pass an exam, succeed in an interview, or obtain a certification. These all share a common requirement: the ability to recall accurate information quickly. In this kind of scenario, Anki is almost the perfect training tool—you are practicing exactly what the task demands: fast and accurate recall. But once you step outside that context, things start to change. What about high-level problem solving? Creative work? Negotiation? Manual tasks? In these cases, recall is only a small part of the process—or sometimes irrelevant altogether. That’s where Anki quickly becomes insufficient, or even useless.

However, there is also a less conventional use for this tool: building long-term memory. The goals may seem smaller or more personal, such as remembering key insights or reinforcing your values. Nevertheless, these still contribute to your overall productivity, and Anki can help you maintain them more efficiently.

There are many tutorials online on how to “master” Anki. However, it is important to remember that creating the cards—not reviewing them—is often the real bottleneck. With some knowledge of HTML, Python, or spreadsheets, you can [automate](/2026/04/06/automation/) parts of the process and generate decks more efficiently. Even so, collecting and organizing the material still takes time, so it is important to plan for this preparation phase before fully committing to Anki.

Anki is a powerful tool for training your mind to respond more quickly and accurately, and to retain information more efficiently. Whether for career advancement or personal growth, if Anki fits both your destination and your path, it is worth using. However, like any tool, it requires preparation. Plan ahead and allocate enough time for setup to avoid wasting time and mental energy. When the right [tools](/2026/04/27/tools/) are used correctly, efficiency naturally improves.
