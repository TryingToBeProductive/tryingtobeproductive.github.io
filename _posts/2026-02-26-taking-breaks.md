*Estimated Reading Time : 3-4min*

We need breaks — we all know that. However, determining how much time should be devoted to intentional rest is tricky. Too little, and you will not feel fully recharged to tackle the next task. Too much, and things simply will not get done. What about mindless entertainment? Even if you watch a movie at 3× speed, it still takes more than half an hour to finish one — time that feels like it could be used better. But unless you have trained yourself sufficiently, it is difficult to keep working without some kind of personal reward, such as consuming content.

If you have tracked your time and managed your tasks for a while, you will quickly notice patterns in your workflow. For example, you may find that for Task A you can focus for 90 minutes and need 30 minutes of rest, while for Task B you can continue for four hours without interruption. With these insights, you can adjust your schedule or your Pomodoro cycles to match your rhythm and take the appropriate amount of rest at the right time. Of course, as you grow older or gain experience, the same task may become easier or harder, allowing you to work for longer or shorter periods. Fortunately, if you consistently [track your time](/2026/02/09/time-management/), you can receive feedback within a week and adjust your plan accordingly.

Now you know how to approach short breaks — but what about longer ones? Most of us want to consume content from time to time. If done in the right amount, it can effectively boost your quality of life. But if you spend too much time on it, guilt creeps in and leaves you worse off than before. To extract the most value from entertainment, planning is required. In this case, using weekly aggregated data from your device usage can serve as a starting point. For example, if you spend three to four hours each week consuming content, consider merging it into a single time block. Not only does this give you enough time to fully immerse yourself in an activity, but it also significantly reduces the number of context switches throughout the week — one of the major causes of productivity disruption.

If you want to make your relaxation time more intentional, you can even create a [to-do](/2026/02/22/to-do-list/) list for it. During the week, whenever you feel the urge to watch videos or play games, write it down. By following that list during your scheduled downtime, you can actually watch or play what you genuinely want instead of mindlessly scrolling.

Breaks, relaxation, and entertainment are clearly important for our well-being. However, when done unintentionally, both our quality of life and productivity decline. Fortunately, by using your own data, you can better plan and manage those periods — and extract more value from your so-called “unproductive” time.
