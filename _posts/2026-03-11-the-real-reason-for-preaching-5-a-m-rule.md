For some reason, whenever morning people talk about how productive they are, they often claim that waking up at 4 or 5 a.m. is the “game-changer.” Depending on your circadian rhythm, you will either have no problem with it or fiercely oppose the idea. Especially for night owls, waking up that early will not necessarily increase performance, so it should not be encouraged. For people, including myself, whose productivity is not hugely affected by time shifts, I would recommend at least trying it. But why does starting your day early allow us to get more things done and feel better about ourselves?

The issue that arises when we begin our day at the same time as everyone else is that there is no so-called “me-time.” When everyone is awake, we have to move along with the rest of society to keep our lives running. Shops need to open, classes need to be prepared, and offices need to be filled on time. Most of our energy is spent reacting to the world rather than giving attention to ourselves. After work, we then deal with extra problems, assignments, or tasks that came from the outside so that we can [restart](/2026/04/10/restarting-systems/) the next day with a clean slate. As a result, even with careful planning and saved mental energy, it is difficult to find a consistent amount of time to push through personal projects or tasks.

When you start your day before the rest of society does, you gain a few consistent, highly controlled hours for yourself. You could spend them on [health](/2026/05/05/health/), creative work, learning, or a complicated project. Once the resource—in this case, time—is stable and disruptions are minimized, objectives are reached and benefits are felt faster. This is the real reason “productivity bros” try to proselytize others to join their “5 a.m. club.”

While reading the previous paragraphs, some readers may have realized that if we shift the time when we start meeting people to later hours, we could receive the same benefits without setting an alarm for five o’clock—which is correct. Of course, certain conditions would have to be met, such as highly flexible work hours, schools starting later, or living mostly alone. But if your circumstances happen to allow it, you should take advantage of your early hours for self-improvement.

To realize our own [dreams](/2026/08/25/expectation/), consistent effort is required. If our biology and environment permit it, using the early hours can provide the time and mental stability needed to accelerate our progress. As a result, both personal productivity and societal responsibilities can receive the attention they deserve.
