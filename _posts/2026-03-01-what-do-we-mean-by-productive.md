Repeating the word “productive” too many times made me stop and think, *what does it actually mean again?* You may have heard of a similar phenomenon called semantic satiation — when repeating a word so often that it temporarily loses its meaning and starts to feel like just a series of sounds.

But beyond this psychological trivia, what do people really mean when they say we need to be “productive”? We can act busy, but that does not automatically translate into increased output. We can take on more jobs, tasks, and activities to involve ourselves in more things, but that often feels like spending time without clear focus. Alternatively, we could maintain the same workload but complete it more efficiently to improve productivity per hour. However, the resulting expansion of free time might make us appear “lazy” to others.

If we check the Oxford Dictionary, “productive” is defined as “resulting in or providing a large amount or supply of something” or “having positive results.” That is more abstract than I expected — which ironically mirrors how the word is often used in real life. So why not create a more specific definition for our own website?

To cover as many situations as possible while remaining specific enough to be useful, we define productivity in our blog as:

> *Being able to take in the desired amount of input and produce the desired amount of output.*

In other words, being satisfied with your input and output.

Based on this definition, you do not have to take on more than you need, nor reduce your tasks to only the bare essentials. You also do not have to produce more than you are capable of, nor stop yourself from surpassing your original goals. As long as you have effective control over your process and feel content with your results, you are being “productive.”

So after you finish reading our blogs, use them to build a system you can rely on and to become your best self. That is the goal of this blog.
