We sometimes make excuses such as "I am not motivated" or "I lack the incentives" for our laziness. Of course, to prevent it, we automate our workflows, turn them into [routines](/2026/02/20/routines/), or redefine our objectives to further reduce our reliance on motivation. However, we tend to forget that there are incentives, both from within and from outside, that encourage us to procrastinate.

Prioritizing energy conservation is an incentive as ancient as life itself. When we don't feel like working, it may also mean that we are in the mood to preserve our stamina for something else in the future. Based on this interpretation, we can significantly reduce this motivation by sleeping, eating, and exercising well, as well as reducing the number of major tasks we take on each day. The former creates an energy surplus, while the latter eliminates the need for saving it.

Things get complicated when there are people who profit from our sluggish behavior. Digital entertainment is built around people who spend their precious time on these platforms. Even in the [analog](/2026/03/26/analog-distraction/) world, newspapers, stores, cafes, and many other places require their customers to not be working and to keep consuming their [time and money](/2026/03/22/budgeting/) in those spaces.

From eye-catching ads, relaxing music, and exciting promises to [FOMO](/2026/03/15/survivorship-bias-and-fomo/), infinite content, and zero barriers to entry, the methods are nothing new. Yet businesses continue to invest their profits in further redirecting people's attention. As such, if you are not in the mood to be productive but will happily scroll through digital or analog windows, you have the energy—but it is being used by others.

As usual, out of sight, out of mind. [Blocking](/2026/02/24/blocking-distractions/) outside influence is the best way to regain control. Being cut off from the world, even if only for a few hours, can be depressing at first. Nevertheless, when your energy starts getting converted into desired results, motivation and satisfaction follow, allowing you to endure the isolation.

Technically speaking, there is no such thing as "not being motivated" or "lacking incentives." However, being motivated not to work and being incentivized to do otherwise are real states that need to be systematically managed. Similar to how electrical work can be controlled by the amount of current, resistance, and direction of flow, our energy to do our work can also be manipulated through those three factors. Trying to be more productive can sometimes mean reducing the amount of fights required to get started. Sadly, the battle never ends, so shorten the list of enemies to lessen your struggles.
