It might be beating a dead horse at this point, but the spread of the "This Is Fine" meme can't be understated. An anthropomorphic dog calmly sipping coffee in a burning room while saying, "This is fine," somehow managed to capture the feeling of billions of people worldwide.

To cut to the chase, productivity systems should prevent such situations from occurring in the first place. [To-do lists](/2026/02/22/to-do-list/) make necessary work and tasks visible, time managers allocate an appropriate amount of time to pursue our lives, work, and ambitions, and personal reviews help us identify problems early and come up with solutions before things get out of hand. When the entire process is performing at its peak, we don't have to live in denial of an incoming disaster because we know what is happening and have taken steps to prevent it.

However, when there is a time lag between implementation and results, we are sometimes forced to ignore the fire temporarily to help us get through it. Responses have to wait, skills have to be developed, and research requires digging into obscure [details](/2026/03/06/details/). Even when we understand that things take time, it is hard to feel as if we are just sitting there without taking direct action to fix the problems. At least you know why you are in a predicament, which is different from knowing that something is wrong without knowing why.

Trying to be productive sometimes means facing the uncomfortable fact that we are far from being in control. Even when we realize that we can't keep making things worse for ourselves, we can't rush through slow improvements. As such, for a limited period, we have to pretend that "This is fine." We can't go into every room and extinguish the fire right away without checking its structural integrity first. Be patient and trust your system, because things WILL be fine when the right steps are planned and being executed.
