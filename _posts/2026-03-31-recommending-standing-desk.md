When we are young, we are conditioned to believe that serious work—such as studying—takes place at a desk with a chair. However, if you look around modern workplaces, more companies and even self-employed individuals are adopting standing desks for deep work. Is this just another productivity trend that makes people *feel* more productive, or does it actually improve performance?

Even if we cannot immediately see the benefits of working upright, we can quickly feel the downsides of sitting for long hours. One well-known occupational hazard of modern work is neck and back pain. No matter how hard we try, it is difficult to maintain perfect posture throughout the day. When we enter a state of flow, we tend to forget that our sitting position is harming us. Only when we regain awareness of our surroundings do we begin to feel the pain we have brought upon ourselves.

Fortunately, when the desk height is properly adjusted, working in an upright position requires almost no effort to keep your spine aligned. Not only does this conserve mental energy for the task at hand, but it also allows you to work longer without discomfort. These benefits alone can significantly increase productivity by improving both the [quality](/2026/08/20/quantity-and-quality/) and quantity of your work.

There is still one issue, however. When you are standing, it becomes much easier to walk away from your task when it gets boring or difficult. This can prolong the time needed to complete demanding work, as your unconscious mind may literally steer you away from it. To counter this, you may need to create a physical anchor before using a standing desk. For example, wired headphones can subtly tether you to your workspace. If you have the [budget](/2026/03/22/budgeting/), a small treadmill can make you think twice before stepping away. Sometimes, we need to deliberately chain ourselves in order to get things done.

Before you rush to buy new equipment, consider postponing your purchase. If your work mainly involves typing and rarely requires handwriting, you can build a makeshift standing desk using stacked cardboard boxes. Buying a new desk also means sacrificing additional space for another piece of furniture. Moreover, if you do not measure your ideal desk height in advance, you may end up with a setup that causes more harm than a regular seated position. Try a free, improvised setup first, and then decide whether it is worth the investment.

Posture can make or break productivity. If we want to avoid long-term work-related injuries, shifting from sitting to standing can reduce unnecessary strain. However, too much freedom can also harm performance, so some restrictions may be beneficial. Small experiments allow for a smoother [transition](/2026/08/13/adjustment-period/) to new working habits. Gather feedback from your own experience by testing a temporary setup. Whether or not you ultimately invest in a standing desk, working upright can meaningfully improve your daily output.
