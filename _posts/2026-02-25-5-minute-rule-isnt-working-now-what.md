*Estimated Reading Time : 3-4min*

One of the first tricks people learn when trying to overcome procrastination is the 5-minute rule: just do the task for five minutes, then decide whether you want to continue. In most cases, once your mind and body have adjusted to the new activity, it becomes easier to keep going than to stop. Our biochemistry is designed to maintain homeostasis, so it is often difficult to interrupt what we are already doing and change direction.

This method may work for a while, but eventually even your brain gets used to the trick. “If I start for five minutes, I will probably end up working for hours — so why not stop myself from starting in the first place?” Our minds are smart. With enough experience, they can anticipate the future and find logical ways to [sabotage](/2026/05/25/capable/) it if necessary. In addition, our bodies are naturally wired to conserve energy. Material wealth and modern productivity standards are relatively recent phenomena in the timeline of human evolution. For most of history, conserving energy rather than working excessively increased the chances of survival. Since our biological systems do not share the same goals as our modern ambitions, it is natural that they sometimes cooperate to resist our “productivity hacks.”

So what should we do when the 5-minute rule has been neutralized?

The solution is to double down — specifically, to lower the hurdle, friction, and expectations even further. The 5-minute rule works initially because it mentally lowers the activation energy required to begin. But there are many other ways to reduce that activation energy. Use hyper-scheduling to allocate generous time blocks so you can work more casually. Use a [task manager](/2026/02/22/to-do-list/) to clear mental clutter. Remove physical obstacles. Stop trying to be perfect from the beginning. Ask others to help you initiate the activity. Allow limited multitasking if it makes transitions easier. And so on.

Of course, unless you somehow reduce the activation energy to below zero, your brain and body will continue trying to conserve energy. But instead of fighting them directly, it may be better to understand their concerns and negotiate with them. [Compromise](/2026/04/02/trade-offs/) where possible. As long as you are doing something, you are already more productive than when you are doing nothing.
