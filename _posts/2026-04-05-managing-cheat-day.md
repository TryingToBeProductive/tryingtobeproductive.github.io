[Blocking](/2026/02/24/blocking-distractions/) distractions is necessary to preserve productivity. However, to make a filter truly effective, we often end up locking out apps or websites that are also useful for improving performance. This includes apps like system settings that grant permissions, or websites such as industry [news](/2026/03/02/news/) that help us understand the current landscape. Even YouTube tutorials can sometimes be more effective than written instructions when problems are difficult to understand.

To deal with this without getting sucked into a vortex of distraction, we can use a “cheat day.” You may have heard this term in the context of maintaining good habits. Most people cannot stay strict with their routines forever. When we start making exceptions on the spot, it sets a precedent for making further excuses, slowly dismantling the habits we tried to build. However, if you deliberately set aside one day each week to “cheat,” it prevents arbitrary exceptions while still giving you a necessary break.

The use case I am proposing here is slightly different, but it functions in a similar way. Once a week, you disable your filters, let apps update, adjust settings, and do research on platforms that are usually unproductive. Of course, you are more likely to get distracted when all protections are removed. That is why limiting this to a single day not only safeguards the rest of the week, but it also accounts for the fact that you will likely get distracted. That’s also why you should write down the tasks you want to complete beforehand—otherwise, you might spend the entire day scrolling without finishing any maintenance work.

You don’t need to remove all of your safeguards, however. Much of this work can be done on a smartphone. While some tasks are easier on a larger screen with a keyboard, those setups also make it easier to browse endlessly or binge content. By limiting exposure, you create physical constraints that help guide your attention in an otherwise distracting environment.

Similar to how programmers deal with technical debt from time to time, we also need to resolve issues caused by strict filters to keep our systems running smoothly. However, if not planned carefully, these “maintenance sessions” can become convenient excuses for distraction. By scheduling a weekly session to handle these specific tasks, you ensure your system stays functional and up to date. In addition, digital chaos can often be managed through physical order, so use hardware to prevent a steep drop in productivity during your cheat day.
