People often talk about money as a way to buy time—to free ourselves so we can focus on more important things. This idea usually appears in the context of chores. If we look at human progress, we can see a pattern that supports this: by delegating tasks to other people or to machines, we free up more time for ourselves.

However, this is only one side of the relationship. People—including myself—also spend money on things that consume our time. We buy objects that require care and attention, subscribe to platforms that keep us entertained, and invest in activities that demand more time than their cheaper alternatives.

Of course, treating ourselves from time to time is not something to look down on. In fact, our well-being needs to be properly maintained in order to sustain high performance. However, when both our time and money are spent at the expense of meaningful work or personal projects, not only does our output in other areas decline, but we also lose the ability to fully enjoy what we paid for—because we remain aware of what we are sacrificing.

As you may have sensed, there needs to be a balance—or at least a limit, depending on how you view it. Fortunately, there is a simple and well-established tool for this.

Budgeting is straightforward: plan ahead how much money you will spend in each category—such as food, daily necessities, education, entertainment, and activities—and then try your best to follow that plan. While it can become more complex if you dive into the [details](/2026/03/06/details/), a reliable budgeting system ultimately helps you stop wasting both time and money on what you merely want, and instead invest your resources in what truly matters. The result is an improvement in both productivity and quality of life.

Time is money, and money is time. Not only are they interchangeable, but they are also spent together. To avoid losing more resources than necessary, we must be intentional about how we spend. Fortunately, budgeting already provides a practical system to support this goal. By planning your finances in advance, you can prevent unnecessary sacrifices and improve overall productivity.
