"The higher they rise, the harder they fall" is a variant of the famous phrase "The bigger they are, the harder they fall," yet the former better captures how we experience letdowns.

Having high hopes and ambitions is a great way to try new things, plan for a better future, and do your best at your work. Still, when you have too high an expectation, the more painful the disappointment becomes. Of course, having such unreasonable demands or desires is not entirely the fault of the individual. Advertisers, gurus, and even genuine people can entice you with too-good-to-be-true promises to buy their products or simply make you feel better.

Managing expectations is difficult, even when you know how to calculate them.

Therefore, if it is hard to control expectations through a single point estimate, they have to be moderated through multiple points systematically.

The first step is to quickly decouple your performance from the highs your dreams give you. Fortunately, the bigger the [goal](/2026/02/13/goal-setting/), the more steps, time, and planning it requires to achieve it. As a result, your actions have to be turned into detailed processes that can be followed and divided into daily time blocks or [routines](/2026/02/20/routines/), so that you can show up to do the work each day without hoping for a big reward. Make your road to success as automatic as possible to reduce your reliance on "motivational self-talk."

After your effort is no longer driven by high hopes, you can start safely lowering your expectations by writing down your mental simulations. As you fill in the [details](/2026/03/06/details/) of your potential outcomes and the actions leading up to them, you tend to realize that some of your speculation makes no sense, at least from your current perspective. After this realization, you can change your plans to increase the likelihood of bigger returns or simply modify your aspirations to fit the existing constraints. Either way, in these situations, you don't need to worry about losing motivation and affecting your daily performance because motivation has already been separated from execution.

Finally, as time progresses, you start to collect real-life data points to check and recalibrate your expectations. It might be a reality check for some, but the potential letdowns won't be too dire. You will keep following your roadmap, and your imagination won't get any wilder. In fact, comparing your dreams with reality over time softens the impact, allowing you to skip the five stages of grief and reach acceptance early and efficiently.

The tension between expectation and activation can't be solved in one step. We have to tackle each of them separately first. Only then can we reconnect them without bringing everything down. [Mood](/2026/03/24/dealing-with-mood-swings/) swings are major productivity disruptors. Hopes and dreams are among the few tools we have to manage our feelings. So, use them carefully to maintain and improve our long term performance indirectly.
