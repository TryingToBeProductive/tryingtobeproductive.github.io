We are constantly reminded of how illogical our brains can be. In my case—and I believe most people have experienced this as well—I see that side of my mind every 24 hours before going to sleep. Sleep is a prerequisite for a productive workday; everyone knows that. Then why would our inner selves try to stay awake longer than necessary? If you search for “bedtime procrastination,” you will find plenty of psychological explanations for this phenomenon. In this blog, however, I will skip that part and go straight to how to deal with it systematically.

As most people have already suspected, digital devices can fuel procrastination, even when what we are avoiding is simply going to sleep. They can be sources of entertainment, work, or learning. As long as there is an activity available, it becomes a short-term excuse to stay awake longer. So the solution is simple: deactivate them before sleep time.

For smartphones, the same apps that [block](/2026/02/24/blocking-distractions/) unproductive phone usage can be used to turn the phone into a brick when taken to the extreme. The advantage of such apps is that they can be scheduled so we do not have to spend any mental energy enforcing the rule ourselves. Set them to block browsers, basic tools, and even [podcast](/2026/03/19/audio-content/) apps. If you cannot access them, you cannot use them.

For computers, the crontab command line can be your ally. Not only can it automatically shut down the device every night, but with a few additional commands you can keep it that way until the next morning. As a result, once your desktop is shut down, you cannot turn it on again no matter how hard you try. Through such measures, you eliminate most late-night activities that would eat away at the next day’s performance.

However, there may still be physical tasks that need to be taken care of before sleep. If you do not plan ahead, they may delay your bedtime. So make a list—or in this case, a checklist. It may include checking doors, doing a night stretch, preparing for the commute, or taking supplements. Combined with [time tracking](/2026/02/09/time-management/), you will have a clear view of what needs to be done and how long everything takes. Once you follow your system, you may be surprised at how difficult it becomes to find excuses to delay a good night’s [rest](/2026/02/26/taking-breaks/).

Procrastination is caused by our psychology. If we cannot reliably fight it with willpower alone, we need to approach the problem asymmetrically. By constructing digital and physical systems that constrain our behavior, we can secure our sleep time—and with it, a more productive day.
